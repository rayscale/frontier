# Voice

Frontier speaks like a precise instrument: neutral, direct, and technical. It does not perform
personality through language. The interface states facts, gives instructions, and gets out of the
way.

## Traits

- **Precise.** Name the exact thing and the exact result.
- **Neutral.** Do not flatter, flatter the brand, or dramatize.
- **Direct.** Use the shortest sentence that carries the meaning.
- **Technical.** Prefer the accurate term over a metaphor.

## Impersonal

The interface speaks impersonally. It does not use "you" or "we".

- Write "The file could not be saved."
- Do not write "We couldn't save your file."
- Do not write "You can try again."

This keeps the interface consistent across products and avoids taking a position on who caused a
problem.

## No performance

The voice never uses humor, exclamations, emojis, or emphasis through capital letters. It does not
blame the person, and it does not apologize repeatedly.

- Write "The connection was lost."
- Do not write "Oops! Something went wrong!"

## One term per concept

Each concept has one term. If the product calls it "delete", the interface does not also call it
"remove" or "erase". Terminology is chosen once and used in labels, messages, documentation, and
error text.

## Case

Use sentence case for labels, headings, and body text. Uppercase is reserved for short acronyms and
established abbreviations. See [Typeface](../foundations/typeface.md).

## Tone by situation

The voice stays neutral, but its intensity follows the situation:

- A confirmation is brief and factual.
- An error is direct and offers a next step.
- A warning states the risk without alarm.
- An empty state is calm and points to the next action.

## Principle

The interface should sound exact, calm, and useful, never playful, dramatic, or personal.
