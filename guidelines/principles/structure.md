# Structure

Structure is how Metric makes an interface legible before any color, border, or icon is added. The
grid, the alignment, and the distance between elements tell a person what belongs together and what
comes next.

## Layout comes first

Hierarchy starts with placement. A heading is a heading because of its position and size, not
because it is bold or colored. A group is a group because its members are closer to each other than
to their surroundings.

## Align to something

Every element aligns to the layout grid, to another element, or to a documented optical correction.
Arbitrary offsets break the rhythm and make the interface look accidental. The grid and breakpoints
are defined in [Layout](../foundations/layout.md).

## Proximity over decoration

Use space to group and separate before reaching for a border, a surface change, or a divider.
Borders are functional and are defined in [Shape](../foundations/shape.md). When spacing already
communicates the relationship, adding a line repeats the message.

## Boundaries are deliberate

A boundary appears when a person must be able to identify an edge: the edge of a field, the edge of
a floating surface, the limit between two regions. It is never added to make an area look finished.

## One reading order

The visual order and the reading order agree. The eye should move through the page in the same order
as the keyboard and the assistive technology. When those orders diverge, the structure is wrong.

## Principle

Structure should make the page readable before a single color, border, or effect is applied.
